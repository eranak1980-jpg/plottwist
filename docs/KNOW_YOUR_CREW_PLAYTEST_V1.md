# PlotTwist: Know Your Crew — Human Playtest V1

## Purpose
Prove the social game loop before building a new app.

## Success test
The concept earns a prototype only if a real group shows all three signals:
1. Players understand the core loop within 30 seconds without a rules lecture.
2. A spontaneous laugh, argument, surprise, or "what?!" happens within the first 2–3 rounds.
3. At the end, at least one player voluntarily wants another round / more questions.

Secondary signals: players care about score, discuss reveals without prompting, and remember earlier answers.

## Group
3–6 friends. Ideal first test: 4 players. 10–12 rounds, approximately 15–25 minutes.

## One-sentence rule
One player answers a question about themselves in secret. Everyone else predicts their answer. Correct predictions earn a point.

Do not explain anything else before Round 1.

## Round flow
1. FOCUS — choose one player as the Subject.
2. QUESTION — read one scenario/question.
3. SECRET ANSWER — Subject chooses privately.
4. PREDICT — everyone else chooses what they think Subject chose.
5. REVEAL — show Subject's answer and who predicted correctly.
6. SCORE — +1 for each correct predictor.
7. SOCIAL BEAT — allow conversation/reaction, but never require an explanation.
8. NEXT — immediately available.

Target time: 45–90 seconds per normal round.

## Recommended 12-round sequence
1. Warm Know Me — easy, funny scenario.
2. Who in the Room — positive social choice.
3. Dilemma — two attractive/awkward options.
4. Know Me — lifestyle / money / travel scenario.
5. Who in the Room — trust or partnership.
6. First Callback — reuse an answer from Rounds 1–5.
7. Predict the Crew — everyone reads the room.
8. Know Me — more revealing but safe.
9. Who in the Room — creates a strong reveal.
10. Callback — deliberately challenge an earlier label.
11. Hero Round — visual-worthy scenario.
12. Final Double Points — high-energy prediction.

Avoid Rank Them in the first human test unless the group is moving very easily.

## First test set
### R1 — instant fantasy
"[Subject] wakes up tomorrow with €1,000,000 that must be spent on themselves. What happens first?"
A. Home / property
B. Long trip
C. Dream car / luxury
D. Quit everything and reinvent life

### R2 — island
"[Subject] must spend one month on a deserted tropical island with ONE person in this room. Who do they choose?"
Answer: one player.
Hero visual candidate: chosen pair happy on island; remaining friends comically left behind.

### R3 — dilemma
"[Subject] must choose one:"
A. Dream job, but in a country they dislike
B. Average job, but in their dream place to live

### R4 — money behavior
"[Subject] receives ₪50,000 tonight and must spend all of it before midnight. Where does most of it go?"
A. Travel / experiences
B. Shopping / luxury
C. Friends / family
D. One ridiculous spontaneous purchase

### R5 — trust
"[Subject] is in real trouble at 3:00 AM. Who in this room do they call first?"
Answer: one player.

### R6 — callback
Generate from a prior answer. Example if R4 = Travel:
"A few rounds ago [Subject] chose travel over everything else. Now the trip goes completely wrong. Who in this room would they trust to rescue the situation?"
Answer: one player.

### R7 — read the room
"Who does the GROUP think could go the longest without their phone?"
Each player predicts who will receive the most votes, then everyone votes.

### R8 — unexpected self
"Which version of [Subject] would surprise the group LEAST?"
A. Opens a restaurant
B. Moves abroad with one suitcase
C. Becomes famous online
D. Disappears for a month without telling anyone

### R9 — partnership
"[Subject] must start a company tomorrow and give one person here 50% ownership. Who gets it?"
Answer: one player.

### R10 — contradiction callback
Use the strongest label learned so far. Example:
"You all treated [Subject] as the responsible one. They now get €20,000 and are FORBIDDEN to save or invest it. What do they do?"
Options generated from their previous answers.

### R11 — Hero visual
"[Subject] wins an all-expenses-paid trip and can take only one person here. Who goes?"
Answer: one player.
Hero visual: cinematic vacation scene with selected pair; humorous reaction from others.

### R12 — double points
"Which choice by [Subject] would the rest of you be MOST wrong about?"
Give four choices personalized from answers already collected. Subject chooses secretly. Correct predictors receive 2 points.

## Crew Memory to record during test
For every answer save a short fact, not a psychological claim.
Examples:
- Eran chose travel when given a windfall.
- Daniel chose Yuval for the island.
- 3/4 players predicted Ron would last longest without a phone.
- Maya chose Noa as her 3AM call.

Never infer sensitive traits or claim the AI "knows" someone's personality. Callbacks should reference actual choices made in the game.

## Host observation sheet
Do not rescue the group unless they are truly stuck.
Record:
- Time until everyone understands the loop.
- First spontaneous laugh/reaction round.
- Any question requiring explanation.
- Any round where phones interrupt conversation instead of triggering it.
- Strongest reveal.
- Weakest question.
- Whether score matters to players.
- Whether callbacks feel surprising/personal or repetitive.
- Whether anyone asks for another round.

## End questions
Ask separately before discussing the product:
1. What was the most fun moment?
2. Which question created the best reaction?
3. Where did the game slow down or become unclear?
4. What would make you want to play again with another group?
5. Would you send/share one of the visual reveals?
6. Would you pay for a polished game night like this? Do not suggest a price first.

## Kill / Continue rule
CONTINUE to coded prototype if the core loop is immediately understood and the group creates social energy without host intervention.
REVISE once if the loop is understood but questions are weak.
STOP this concept if players still need repeated instructions, reveals produce little conversation, or the group treats it like a questionnaire rather than a game.
